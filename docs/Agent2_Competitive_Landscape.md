## 2. WHAT THEY DO RIGHT AND WRONG

### RIGHT
- Radical accessibility (no coding prerequisites)
- Practical, tool-first approach
- Community + accountability (Lenny's 30K+ Slack community)
- Consistent daily cadence
- Editorial independence builds trust

### WRONG
- Assumed technical baseline (even "beginner" courses)
- Theory-heavy, action-light
- Pace mismatch for older learners
- **"Bro Culture" energy** -- most AI YouTube channels 93-96% male audience
- Information overload without curation
- No real-time support for beginners
- Rapid obsolescence (courses outdated in 6 months)

## 5. THE 35-65 DEMOGRAPHIC GAP

**This is the single most underserved segment in AI education.**

- Only 7% of workers 50+ have taken AI training classes

- 3 in 5 adults 50+ say technology is NOT designed with their age in mind

## 6. BRANDING THAT WORKS

### DO:
- Position learner as the hero, not the student
- Frame AI as augmenting their expertise
- Use warm, confident language
- Show real people their age using AI successfully
- Use warm colors and high-contrast design

### DO NOT:
- Fear-based messaging ("AI will take your job if you don't...")
- Youth-coded language ("hustle," "grind," "10x," "level up")
- Tiny text, low-contrast, cluttered interfaces
- Frame learning as remediation ("catch up," "don't get left behind")

### The "Active Agers" Market
- 75M+ Americans identified as "Active Agers"
- 92% bank online, 91% own smartphones, 84% post on social media
- 52% are open to trying new brands
- This is NOT a technophobic audience. They are an UNDERSERVED audience.
